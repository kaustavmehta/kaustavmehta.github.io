Modeled the accumulation and degradation of Amyloid beta in Alzheimer's disease as a function of neuroinflammation, initially by simulating an existing deterministic system of hundreds of differential equations (COPASI). Developed scripts to parallelize parameter scans, exponentially reducing computation time and enabling in-depth exploration of model behavior. Adapted the model using STEPS into a stochastic diffusion system with reaction-mixture properties to investigate the impact of microscopic stochasticity on global system states. Worked on designing a de-novo, first-principles model that leverages literature data to more accurately capture the biochemical and biophysical dynamics underlying Amyloid beta aggregation. Analyzed and interpreted high-dimensional simulation data to gain insights into disease mechanisms.
  • My contributions towards the initial understanding of the complex system and reverse engineering the COPASI model into its constituting coupled differential equations significantly laid the groundwork for multiple student capstone projects in the Biological Sciences, and ultimately culminated in a Nature npj Systems Biology and Applications (2024) paper titled "Bifurcations in coupled amyloid-β aggregation-inflammation systems".
